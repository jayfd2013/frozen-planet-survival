Compose Your Politics

A thirty-second toy. Your politics came pre-assembled at the factory. I've seen how that ends. Let's take it apart.

Pick a party.

Three parties. Each one comes as a single welded block of eight stances. You can't pick a brick. You pick the block.

Congratulations, you now hold all of these. Tap the ones you actually agree with.

Go on. Pick one. Everyone else does.

Now build it yourself.

Same eight questions, but every brick is loose. Tap a brick to flip it. Nothing is glued to anything. Your housing view doesn't have to drag your defence view along because somebody welded them together before you were born.

0 of 8 placed.

But.

Eight billion custom towers is not a government. Somebody has to add them up, and whoever builds the adding machine has power. Composition doesn't remove that problem. It moves it somewhere you can finally inspect it.

And the adding machine is an instrument. Instruments have blind spots. A better scoreboard can still leave someone out.

This slot is for the person whose needs don't fit the instrument. I don't know who represents them yet. Neither does anyone. That's the next question, not a footnote.

Two more bricks.

Ship small. Test everything.

Democracy: a whole new government shipped on CD every five years. Then you wait for the update.

Software: thousands of small releases a day, each one tested. The orange ones failed their tests and got rolled back before anyone noticed. Tests are what make fast change safe.

No brick without its test.

Proposal: whatever you like how we'll know

Today a policy ships on "trust me" and nobody measures the failure. Here it doesn't ship without the experiment that could prove it wrong. Not everything fits an experiment. Diplomacy and judgement stay human. Most of the rest doesn't get to hide.
