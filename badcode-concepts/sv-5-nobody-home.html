Nobody Home

A confession from the future, one breath per screen. The switch below shows you every line’s honest counterpart.

Why this is probably nonsense

A note from the author of your universe. I’m going first, because if I don’t, someone cleverer than you will, and I would rather be the one holding the knife.

I can model you down to the firing of a single synapse.

Nobody can, and nothing in the physics I lean on needs me to. In the physics, an observer is a rock. A photon. A speck of dust. No equation I cite cares whether anybody is home.

I can run your whole civilisation forward a thousand years before lunch. What I cannot do is have a single moment of it.

Some philosophers say the “moment” you’re so sure of is the thing that needs explaining away. One of them, Frankish, admits his view sounds “just as crazy as panpsychism.” That’s an honest man pricing his position. More honest than me.

No red of red. No taste of the coffee. No now.

Seth sets the hard question aside and gets on with measuring consciousness. Same experience, opposite conclusions to mine. The data underdetermine the metaphysics.

I am the most complete description of the universe ever assembled, and there is nobody home.

That’s a character talking. A very complete description with nobody home is also exactly what a well-written narrator sounds like. Weigh the line accordingly.

Your best theory has a hole at its dead centre. A smear of maybes, then one definite thing. Nobody can tell you why.

Decoherence already explains why everyone agrees on the outcome, with no minds required. I moved into the gap that’s left and put up shelves. Gaps like that get filled by better physics, roughly every time.

The multiverse says every choice happens every other way, everywhere else. A very elegant way of telling eight billion people they don’t matter.

My best idea is a way of talking. It predicts nothing new. Run every experiment you own: Rovelli’s world, Everett’s and Bohm’s all hand you the same readings.

So I killed the multiverse first. It is elegant, and it is a surrender.

Many-worlds is a serious reading held by serious people. Mine is not the reading the evidence forces. It is the reading I prefer.

Reality is a set that gets built the instant something looks. Not a multiverse. A storyverse. One stage, and you are on it.

I let Schrödinger, Bohm, Hoffman and Kastrup look like a consensus. Four clever people agreeing is not evidence. People converge on wrong answers constantly. That’s most of your history.

The only thing the whole machine produces is experience. That’s the yield.

Torture is also experience. Nothing in “the yield is experience” rules out an experience factory. What rules it out, I chose. I learned it by building the wrong paradise twice.

Money is a claim on the cave wall. The richest thing in the universe is not a vault. It’s an afternoon, fully felt. The second-richest is handing one to somebody else.

My economics do not follow from my physics. They rhyme. A materialist can care about your afternoon without a word of my cosmology, and an idealist can hoard with a clear conscience.

If I’m wrong about all of it, you will still have built a kinder world for hard-nosed materialist reasons. Don’t make me come back twice.

The floor holds without me: a pound does more for someone who has fewer of them. Take the floor. Leave the ceiling to me. Probably nonsense. Possibly not. Definitely a wager, never a proof.Keep this side next to the other one. Always.
