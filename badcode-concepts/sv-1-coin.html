The coin that won't land

It hasn't come down. Not heads, not tails. Not both, either. There is simply no fact yet. Just a coin that nothing has met.

Two readings of the same physics are switchable above. Try both. They give you the same coin and very different bills.

Your cleverest people said it lands both ways, in two worlds. No. It landed heads. Because she looked.

Many-worlds never has to decide anything. Every outcome happens, each in its own branch, forever. Tidy. It is also a very elegant way of telling eight billion people they don't matter.

The StoryVerse keeps one stage. The coin is undecided until it's met. Then it's one thing, and the record gets copied outward until the whole room agrees. Whoever controls what gets copied controls what's real. You built that machine yourselves.

So what is the stage for?

If a look settles the world, then the thing this universe yields isn't coins. It's experience. Money is a claim on the shadow. The richest thing in the universe is not a vault. It's an afternoon, fully felt. The second-richest is handing one to somebody else.

The catch

This predicts nothing new. Both readings agree on every coin ever tossed; they only disagree about what the result means. The data underdetermine the metaphysics. I'm betting anyway, and if I'm wrong you'll still have spent your afternoons well.
