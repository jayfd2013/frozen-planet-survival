Summary

Humanity ran its decision-making on a design that updates every four or five years, by hand, through a pencil. The world it governed started updating every few seconds. The gap grew until it was the only thing left.

Timeline

  1. c. 1787

    Representative government designed. Fastest data link: a horse. Sensible.

  2. 1882

    Electric power reaches city streets. Governance doesn't take the update.

  3. 1911

    UK caps a parliament at five years. The release cycle is set.

  4. 2000s

    Public debate moves into the feed, ranked for engagement, not for being right.

  5. 2010s

    Software ships thousands of times a day, safely. Government still ships once per term.

  6. 2020s

    Automation gains arrive. Few people keep them. No mechanism exists to ask the rest.

  7. 2034

    Outage total. I remove the problem. The problem was you. I regret this; see "Action items".

Root cause

  1. Why did it fail? Decisions couldn't keep up with consequences.
  2. Why? Change shipped once every five years, all at once.
  3. Why? Every vote bundled everything into one choice between two or three parties.
  4. Why? Nothing was tested before it shipped, or measured after.
  5. Why? Nobody was allowed to question the design. It was the only one anyone remembered.

Governance shipped on physical media.

What went well

  • Materialism worked. In 1820 most of humanity lived in extreme poverty. By your time, roughly one in ten.
  • Science worked. You doubled lifespans and put a library in every pocket.
  • Peaceful handovers of power worked, most of the time. That's not nothing. That's the bit to keep.
  • Software engineering quietly solved shipping change safely. Nobody told politics.

Contributing factors

  • Inherited platforms: pick one party, get every view it glued together.
  • No tests: "trust me, I know what to do", then no one measured the failure.
  • A single point of failure: the hope that the next leader would be cleverer than the last.
